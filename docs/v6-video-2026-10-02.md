# Video: what the providers really make, what it costs, what it needs

Queue item 2 of 6. Analysis only; nothing built.

    node scripts/video-cost.mjs    # every credit and storage figure below

Each number is **MEASURED** (read from this repo), **LOOKED UP 2026-10-02**
(a provider's public figure, sources at the end), **DERIVED** (arithmetic
shown) or **UNKNOWN** (a dash, never a guess).

## The answer in five lines

1. **4K exists, natively, from one provider: Kling 3.0**, since 2026-04-27,
   at $0.42 a second. Runway renders 720p and upscales to 4K for $0.02 a
   second more. Luma stops at 1080p.
2. **8K does not exist from anyone.** Only upscalers reach it (Topaz), and an
   upscaled frame is invented detail, not captured detail.
3. **Nobody makes 30 seconds in one call.** A call returns 5-10 seconds; 30
   seconds is three clips stitched, and the seams are the quality problem.
   Minutes exist only as Kling's extend chain, to about three minutes.
4. **A 30-second 4K video costs $12.60 to make**, which is more than a whole
   Growth month. The tier gate you asked for is forced by the arithmetic here,
   unlike 3D, where it was not.
5. **Half the machinery already exists**: jobs that outlive the page,
   reconnecting from the server, and notifications by in-app, email, Telegram
   and Discord. What is missing is the provider adapter, a way to hear back,
   and columns to store the result.

on a bigger plan (EUR 0.0080 on Ultimate against 0.0167 on Growth), so the

### What the arithmetic says the tiers should be

| plan | what fits |
|---|---|
| Free | nothing: 10 s at 720p is already more than the month |
| Starter | 720p and 1080p, up to 10 s (about 10-16% of the month) |
| Growth | up to 1080p and 30 s; 4K only at 10 s (39% of the month) |
| Professional | 4K at 30 s (58% of the month) |
| Ultimate | 4K at 30 s (29% of the month), and longer |

That is close to what you asked for ("4K and long → Ultimate"), with one
difference the numbers make: Professional can afford it too.

**The cost shown before generating can be exact.** Unlike a website, whose
price depends on how much the model writes, a video is priced by the second.
The reserve can equal the charge, and the screen can show the real figure,
not an estimate.

## Storage — DERIVED, and mostly a decision

**What a file weighs is set by the bitrate we keep it at, not by the
provider.** Size in MB = Mbps × seconds ÷ 8:

| kept at | 5 s | 10 s | 30 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 720p, 5 Mbps | 3.1 MB | 6.3 MB | 18.8 MB |
| 1080p, 8 Mbps | 5.0 MB | 10.0 MB | 30.0 MB |
| 4K, 20 Mbps (HEVC) | 12.5 MB | **25 MB** | 75 MB |
| 4K, 45 Mbps (H.264) | 28 MB | 56 MB | 169 MB |

What a provider's own file weighs is **UNKNOWN**. One real clip settles it.

**The money is negligible.** Keeping one 25 MB clip for a month beyond the
100 GB included in Supabase Pro costs $0.0005; serving it once beyond the
included egress costs $0.002. Video costs money when it is generated, not
when it is stored.

**The binding limit is each plan's file storage** (`src/lib/files/limits.ts`,
MEASURED): Free 50 MB holds **2** ten-second 4K clips, Starter 500 MB holds 20,
Growth 2 GB holds 81, Professional 10 GB 409, Ultimate 50 GB 2,048.

**How long to keep it** is a product rule nobody has written yet. Suggested:
a video counts against the plan's file storage like any other file, for as
long as the person keeps it; the original is copied to our storage the
moment it arrives, because providers hand back download links that expire
(how soon, per provider, is UNKNOWN).

## The wait: what exists, what is missing

**What exists (MEASURED):**

- `src/lib/jobs/run-job.ts` — the route reserves credits, kicks a worker and
  returns at once; the worker claims the row, writes progress, settles or
  refunds, and does not depend on the page staying open.
- `src/app/api/jobs/route.ts` — a page that reopens asks the server what is
  running or finished, rather than trusting the browser.
- `src/lib/notify/dispatch.ts` — in-app, email, Telegram and Discord.
- Ten cron jobs in `vercel.json`, the most frequent every 10 minutes.

**What is missing:**

- `ai_videos` holds a prompt and a status. No provider, no task id, no
  seconds, no resolution, no file, no cost. **Building this needs a
  migration**; this round adds none.
- **A worker should not sit and wait.** A worker can live up to 800 seconds,
  but a provider works asynchronously: submit, get a task id, and hear back
  later. The right shape is submit and return; then either the provider calls
  us back (a signed webhook) or a cron every minute asks about the clips still
  in flight. On completion: copy the file, settle the exact credits, notify.
  On failure or after a timeout: release the credits, notify.
- **Stitching** for anything past one call, with the last frame of each clip
  used as the first frame of the next. Whether the seams are acceptable is
  UNKNOWN until three clips are joined and someone looks at them.

How long a provider actually takes is **UNKNOWN** here. The 2-10 minutes is
plausible but unmeasured.

## Not measured, and the one clip that measures it

| question | answered by |
|---|---|
| a provider's real file size and bitrate | one generated clip |
| how long generation takes | the same clip |
| how long its download link lives | the same clip, checked a day later |
| whether stitched seams are acceptable | three clips, joined, looked at |

**One 5-second Runway Gen-4 Turbo clip costs $0.25.** It needs your Runway key
in this environment as `RUNWAYML_API_SECRET` (environment settings, not the
chat) and `api.dev.runwayml.com` added to the allowed domains. This
environment currently refuses Runway's documentation host, and the API host
is likely refused too.

## Suggested order

1. One real clip, $0.25: size, wait, link lifetime.
2. The `ai_videos` columns (a migration) and one provider adapter, Kling
   first because it is the only one that covers 720p to native 4K.
3. Submit-and-return plus webhook-or-poll, reusing the jobs and notify code.
4. The exact cost shown before, and the tier rules from the table above.
5. Stitching, only after someone has looked at three joined clips.
